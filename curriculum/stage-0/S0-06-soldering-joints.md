# S0.6 — Soldering I: fifty joints
**Module M0.3 · 🛠️ bench · ~3 h · objectives 0.3.1, 0.3.2, 0.3.3, 0.3.4**

## Why this session
Craft, bought in hours rather than understanding. The syllabus says your brother will likely
outpace you here — let him, and film it.

## Before you start
- ⚠️ **Hazard — this is the first real one. Do not start without all of it:**
  - **Burns / fire:** the tip runs at ~300–350 °C. Iron always in its stand; never put it down on
    the bench; switch off when you leave the room. Hold it like a pen, by the grip only.
  - **Eyes:** clipped leads and solder spatter fly. **Eye protection on.**
  - **Fumes and lead:** flux smoke is an irritant — open a window and run a fan blowing *across*
    the bench, away from your face. If your solder is leaded: no food or drink at the bench, wash
    hands after.
  - If this kit isn't on the bench, **the session is blocked** (red line 3).
- **Have:** temperature-controlled station, 0.6–0.8 mm rosin-core solder, flux, damp sponge or
  brass wool, practice perfboard, resistor offcuts or header pins to solder.
- **Write answers first:** Q0.3.a–d.

## Steps
1. **Set up.** Set ~330 °C to start (adjust per your solder's datasheet). Tin the tip — melt a
   little solder onto it — so it's shiny. A dull, black tip won't transfer heat; clean and re-tin.
2. **The joint, in order** (0.3.1): tip touches **both** the pad and the lead → count ~1–2 s →
   feed solder into the joint (the *pad/lead*, not the tip) → remove solder → remove iron. Total
   ~3 s. Good joint: shiny, concave "volcano" fillet, lead outline visible.
3. **Joints 1–20:** resistor leads through perfboard. Photograph every five.
4. **Make the bad ones on purpose** (0.3.2) so you can recognise them: a **cold** joint (move the
   lead while it cools), a **starved** joint (too little solder), a **blob** (too much), a
   **bridge** (two pads joined), an **overheated** joint (dwell 8+ s — note the burnt flux, and the
   pad that might lift).
5. **Flux** (0.3.4). Do five joints with extra flux and five without. Write the difference.
6. **Temperature and mass** (0.3.3). Solder one joint onto a large copper area (or a thick wire)
   at your normal setting. It won't flow — why? Raise the temperature or use a bigger tip and try
   again (Q0.3.b).
7. **Joints 21–50.** Clean ones now.

## Done when
- Fifty joints done and photographed.
- **The five worst are annotated**: what's wrong, and the cause (that's part of M0.3's object).
